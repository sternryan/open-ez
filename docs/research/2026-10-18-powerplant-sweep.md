# Long-EZ powerplant sweep (public sources only)

Searched 18 October 2026. Every figure below is tied to a URL. A cell marked **unsourced** was not found in the pages fetched. Derived figures are labeled and use only the cited inputs.

Mission used in section 5, as specified: a round-trip leg of about **300 nm** (task assumption, not re-measured), two seats, about **150 kt** cruise, plus **30 min** reserve. The great-circle distance was not independently verified.

Plans context, from a transcription of the Rutan Long-EZ owner's manual: approved engines are the Lycoming O-235 (115 hp) and Continental O-200 (100 hp); the O-235 with a 100-octane dynafocal mount is called the most desirable; heavier or higher-horsepower engines are not recommended because of weight, balance, and structure. Fuel is two 26 gal tanks (52 gal total). Max true cruise quoted for the Lycoming is 161 kt (185 mph) at 75% power, full throttle, 8,000 ft density altitude. Source: [manualzz transcription](https://manualzz.com/doc/33844055/rutan-long-ez-aircraft-owner%E2%80%99s-manual).

## 1. Combustion engines, about 100–180 hp

Weight notes matter for a tail-mounted pusher. “Dry” and “installed / ready-to-fly” are not the same, and Lycoming’s “standard dry” weight already includes the starter and alternator. Deltas versus the plans engine use Lycoming’s standard weights: O-235-C2C **244 lb**, O-235-L2A **252 lb** ([O-235 operator’s manual, detail weights](https://www.lycoming.com/sites/default/files/file/2025-02/60297-9%20-%20O-235%20and%20O-290%20Series.pdf)).

### 1.2 ULPower (current manuals index)

Cooling, ignition, injection, and drive are common: four-stroke, horizontally opposed, dual electronic ignition, multipoint EFI, **direct propeller drive**, **ram-air-cooled** cylinders and heads. Fuel on the current UL350i page: mogas minimum 95 RON / 87 MON (91 AKI); 100LL if mogas is unavailable; serials above 223501 mapped for 91 RON / 87 AKI. **Ethanol percent: unsourced.** TBO, all current serials above 250000: **2000 h or 12 years**; aerobatic and turbo **1500 h or 12 years**. Older serials below 250000: 1500 h / 12 years, aerobatic and turbo 1200 h / 12 years ([ULPower TBO FAQ](https://ulpower.com/en/engines/faq/ulpower-engine-tbo)).

## 5. Fuel or energy mass for 300 nm at ~150 kt, plus 30 min

### Assumptions (all stated)

1. **300 nm / 150 kt = 2.00 h**, plus **0.50 h** reserve, so **2.50 h** at the cruise power used for 150 kt. Climb, descent, and alternate are not added.
2. Shaft power is scaled from the Long-EZ manual: **161 kt** true at **75%** of the **115 hp** Lycoming, so **86.25 hp** at 161 kt. Assuming power varies with speed cubed (same airplane, same cooling drag): `(150/161)^3 × 86.25 = 69.8 hp (52.0 kW)`. This is a calculation, not a flight test. A radiator, intercooler, or a buried turbo will need **more** power than this. That increment is **unsourced**.
3. Avgas density **5.97 lb/US gal**, **0.715 kg/L**, **43.71 MJ/kg** (Chevron review, “aviation gasoline” typical) ([Skybrary copy](https://skybrary.aero/sites/default/files/bookshelf/2478.pdf)). Jet kerosine typical in the same table: **6.76 lb/US gal**, **43.28 MJ/kg**. Swift’s UL94 figure of 5.87 lb/gal is slightly lower and was not used in the table.
4. Where the only published fuel flow is at a higher power, fuel is scaled with **constant BSFC** down to 69.8 hp. That overstates economy if the real BSFC gets worse at low power, and understates it if the engine is already published near 70 hp.
5. Gasoline mass uses the Chevron avgas density even when the engine is running mogas. Mogas density: **unsourced**.
6. Electric row uses shaft energy divided by EMRAX’s **“up to 96%”** claim. Propeller efficiency is already inside the 69.8 hp, because that horsepower is engine shaft power in the manual’s cruise point. Inverter loss beyond the motor percentage: **unsourced**. No battery reserve beyond the 30 minutes is included; a real pack would keep energy unusable at the bottom of the discharge.
